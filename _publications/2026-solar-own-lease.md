title: "Willingness to Pay for Residential Rooftop Solar Systems: Evidence on Ownership versus Leasing Contracts"

abstract: >
The availability of alternative financing arrangements has fundamentally reshaped the residential rooftop solar market in the United States (U.S.). However, empirical evidence on households’ preferences and willingness to pay (WTP) for solar ownership versus leasing remains limited. This study provides new evidence on household preferences and WTP for solar adoption by explicitly comparing ownership and leasing contracts within a stated-preference framework. We designed a Best-to-Worst Discrete Choice Experiment (BWDCE) that presents different solar options. We implement a nationwide online survey using Qualtrics and collect data from 1,134 U.S. homeowners who have not adopted rooftop solar. We use a mixed logit, latent class, and rank-ordered logit model to estimate households' preferences and WTP for solar systems, allowing for unobserved preference heterogeneity. The preliminary results suggest that homeowners’ first-best choice is solar ownership, whereas their second-best choice is leasing. On average, they are willing to pay a total cost of around $23,707 to own a solar system and $18,449 under a leasing agreement (with incentives). This is closely aligned with the average cost of a 7-14 kW solar system (with incentives) in most U.S. states. The respondents' adoption decisions are primarily driven by financial, system attributes, socio-demographics, political ideology, and party affiliation. Lastly, respondents place greater disutility on upfront down payments relative to equivalent total expected savings, implying implicit discount rate ranges of 0.70-4.30%. This study contributes to the literature on residential solar adoption by providing a comprehensive, contract-specific WTP estimate that distinguishes between ownership and leasing decisions.
